# Photon-conserving alternatives: primary-source audit

Research snapshot: 2026-10-23. Production `global-budget` is unchanged. This is a survey of major method families, not a proof that all imaginable algorithms have been exhausted. Source metadata and retrieval status are in `sources.json`.

## What the literature actually supports

The most immediately useful comparator is **modern SCRIPT**, rather than its much slower 2018 implementation. A second promising research direction is a **conservative positive-kernel allocator with an additive 21cmFAST source adapter**, explicitly tested against SCRIPT; its ingredients have substantial prior art. A third, higher-fidelity direction is **angular packet transport with splitting/merging**, using ARTIST/FlexRT/TRAPHIC as references. It retains direction information that scalar redistribution discards, but no inspected paper establishes a small overhead relative to Qin20 21cmFAST.

These are research recommendations, not measured rankings. “Correct total photons”, “unique bounded spatial allocation”, and “correct directional radiation transport” are different requirements. A method can satisfy either of the first two and fail the third. Existing ES-based and moment-based approximations are not exact RT solvers merely because their photon totals agree.

## Main families

| Family | Conserved or controlled quantity | Native ES barrier retained? | Main limitation / relevant next test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Park redshift calibration; current global-budget baseline | Global history/ledger target | Yes | No source-to-sink assignment or directional guarantee |
| Stochastic halo partition / analytic bubbles | Explicit stochastic source mass in each constructed bubble | Analytic ES organization | Not a 3D field solver; realistic correlated initial conditions complicate partition consistency |
| Modern SCRIPT | Photon-weighted ionized atoms, with overlap photons recycled | No; source-centred shell filling replaces flagging | Spherical redistribution forgets original photon direction; test angular overlap and shadows |
| BEARS / GRIZZLY / BEORN | Overlap budget in painted profiles | No | Boundary expansion/profile assumptions; density and sink accounting must match the conserved measure |
| FFRT-P + modified MFP filters | Additive source representation and attenuated filtering, **not PC by itself** | Yes, modified | Need an explicit allocation ledger; ordinary barrier overlap still duplicates or loses budget |
| AMBER / rank matching | Prescribed mass-weighted ionization history | No | Global ranking is not a source-resolved photon transport solution |
| Positive FFT allocation / constrained transport | Potentially exact algebraic source and cell budgets | Can use ES to guide scales | A new closure must still pass blocking, angular memory, locality, and density tests |
| ARTIST / angular packets | Absorptions plus surviving photons; approximate direction | No | Angular mixing and time stepping; measured cost far above ES |
| C²-Ray / adaptive rays / FlexRT | Photon loss along rays coupled to gas chemistry | No | Source/ray/time-step cost and angular convergence; merging trades noise against cost |
| M1 / OTVET / FLD moments | Conservative radiation moment equations, subject to chemistry solver | No | Angular closure errors; source-count independence does not imply ES-like cost |
| Monte Carlo / TRAPHIC / SimpleX | Packet counts under conservative interactions | No | Angular/discretization errors and sampling cost; fixed angular bins merge different sources |
| Learned emulators | Training-model observables | Indirectly | Speeding inference does not make underlying transport more accurate or per-realization conservative |

## Source-specific findings

**Park, Greig & Mesinger 2022 [PGM22].** Calibrates the redshift delay caused by ES photon loss against an analytic ionization-history model, then shifts the ES ionization calculation in redshift. This preserves the ES topology at a matched reionization stage and is inexpensive, but it does not construct source-resolved allocations or repair angular propagation. The paper explicitly describes approximate conservation. It is the relevant prior baseline for a global history correction, distinct from a locally conservative transport solver.

**Paranjape, Choudhury & Padmanabhan 2016 [P16].** The correct arXiv identifier is **1512.01345**. The attached `1512.00562v2` is Mutch et al.’s Meraxes paper. P16 partitions concentric disjoint mass shells into stochastic halo lists using Sheth–Lemson, then records the largest enclosing sphere whose explicit source budget crosses the ionization threshold. The partition construction is rigorous for white-noise initial conditions; realistic CDM removes independence of disjoint regions. This addresses a conceptual failure of mean collapse fractions, but is not a drop-in 3D transport prescription. Even the paper’s bubble-population results should not be advertised as exact global conservation in realistic CDM. No comparable 21cmFAST runtime benchmark was identified.

**Choudhury & Paranjape 2018 [CP18].** First build each source bubble independently, filling equal-distance shells and allowing overlap cells to exceed unit ionization. Then treat overlap excess as new effective sources and fill remaining neutral capacity. The original sequential second pass is explicitly order-dependent; the authors find the effect mainly near the grid scale. Tests include two equal sources and convergence of large-scale HI bias. Small ES photon discrepancies can accompany much larger power/bias discrepancies; this does not predict a universal sign of power suppression. Original default maps were 100³ cells in a 500 h⁻¹ cMpc box: up to four hours per map versus under one second for ES, precomputed density/halo fields, one laptop core. **This timing and ordering criticism must not be assigned to modern SCRIPT.**

**Optimized SCRIPT, Choudhury, Paranjape & Bosman 2021 [CPB21].** Section 2.1 reports **2–4 seconds for a 128³ ionization map**, approximately 0.01 seconds for 32³, one processor. This is a map-generation claim, not an end-to-end Qin20 comparison. Its Lyα application adds a separate neutral-island shadow prescription. It provides important evidence that conservative overlap redistribution need not inherently cost hours; benchmark the actual implementation on our inputs before assigning an overhead factor.

**Maity & Choudhury 2022 [MC22].** Footnote 2 updates SCRIPT to synchronous rounds: all excess cells redistribute independently using the old field; newly overionized cells are handled in later rounds. The authors explicitly state that this gives a unique map and eases parallelization. Cumulative recombinations increase cell capacity from H to H+R. Thus synchronous redistribution and recombination capacities are established ideas. The reported 6–7 seconds for an entire z=20→5 history with Δz=0.1 uses a 256 h⁻¹ cMpc box and **16 h⁻¹ cMpc cells, i.e. 16³ ionization cells**, not 256³. The paper omits cosmic-dawn X-ray fluctuations, but that is no longer a statement about all SCRIPT versions.

**Later SCRIPT [CC25, RC26].** The 2025 sink extension connects density, clumping, self-shielding, mean free path, and photoionization rate. Its §2.5 explicitly acknowledges that spherical opacity averaging lets an absorber in one direction affect other directions; it compares this approximation to simplified rays. The August 2026 cosmic-dawn preprint adds Lyα and X-ray/spin-temperature fluctuations, with source-centric and sink-centric radiation calculations. Default grid is 16³ in 256 h⁻¹ cMpc; its coarse-grid comparisons do not establish accuracy at Qin20 scales. Neither extension proves exact angular propagation for overlap photon redistribution.

**BEARS / GRIZZLY [T09, G18].** Paint precomputed 1D radiative profiles around halo sources. BEARS identifies connected overlaps and enlarges the corresponding spheres to account for the overlap photon budget. GRIZZLY compares against C²-Ray; quoted 5.5 CPU hours for its reionization history excludes construction of its profile library. These are different source models, resolutions, and workloads from Qin20, so that number is not a slowdown factor. Source profiles and overlap repair replace the ES barrier and approximate inhomogeneous transport.

**BEORN [SGS23].** Uses halo-mass-bin profile painting, then connected components and a Euclidean distance transform to send excess ionization to nearest external boundary layers, with fractional last layers. This is direct prior art for connected-bubble redistribution. Equation 16 grows bubble volume using mean hydrogen density; §2.6.2 redistributes summed ionization fractions/overlap volume. Therefore its conservation guarantee is for that mean-density profile model, not automatically the spatially varying hydrogen-number ledger required here. Appendix A accelerates the method by pooling excess from disconnected small islands over their combined boundaries, sacrificing locality. For a 256³ grid, 147 cMpc box, 7.5 million haloes, and mean ionization 0.5, reported overlap processing falls from 52 minutes to 2 minutes when islands below 40 cells are grouped; the ionization-power change is below 1% over the reported 0.1–1 Mpc⁻¹ range. This is a within-method optimization, not an ES slowdown ratio. Original source directions are not tracked.

**Davies & Furlanetto 2022 [DF22].** MFP-ε modifies the ES threshold using uniform attenuation; MFP-ε(r) filters an emissivity field using an exponentially attenuated real-space top-hat. Their **FFRT-P** computes 21cmFAST collapse fraction once at pixel scale and filters the resulting collapsed-mass field like DexM. This is prior art for an additive halo-free source adapter, and directly relevant to the Qin20 source audit. It is not equivalent to recomputing the native conditional source expectation at every radius. The paper flags photon non-conservation as a remaining caveat. A modified positive filter alone is therefore not the desired PC solution.

**Hassan et al. 2017 [H17].** The time-integrated SimFast21 model improves cumulative ionization/recombination bookkeeping while retaining ES flagging. Its own photon-conservation analysis finds substantial discrepancies and compares centre versus whole-sphere flags. Time integration alone does not repair spatial double spending; it is not an independently conserving alternative.

**AMBER [T22].** Sort an approximate radiation field and assign reionization redshifts by matching cumulative ionized mass to a supplied mass-weighted history. This efficiently separates history from morphology and is a useful rank-based control. It does not establish that photons reaching individual cells satisfy ray attenuation, local source budgets, or a shared sink ledger. Matching the prescribed history should not be reported as a directional conservation proof.

**ARTIST, Molaro et al. 2019 [M19].** This is directional shell RT, not an ES-only correction. Each source’s shell photons carry direction labels; survivors advance to the next shell section. In multiple-source cells, aggregate survivors are divided according to incident photon shares. Weak components transition to 26 background directions. Six source cones were used in reported tests; angular refinement is available. It conserves photon count while approximating directionality, and tests spheres, shadows and cosmological RT. Section 5 compares L=250 Mpc,128³ runs: 12,000 single-core minutes for ARTIST versus 20 for 21CMMC, with 160 versus 80 redshift samples: 600× total, 300× per sample. This is stronger evidence than the abstract’s broad “two orders” description; it is not an end-to-end Qin20 benchmark.

**Rays and packets [C2, F24, PS08, SX10].** C²-Ray uses finite-volume photon absorption and time-averaged optical depths along short characteristics. FlexRT uses HEALPix rays, adaptive splitting, and merging of approximately aligned rays, with a tunable subgrid opacity mode. Its standard tests include dense-clump shadows; coarse moving-screen fronts require unresolved front widths. TRAPHIC merges packets in angular cones to avoid source-count scaling; SimpleX adds direction-conserving transport to its graph formulation. These provide a controlled route to retaining angular memory. None inspected establishes modest overhead versus native ES at the required Qin20 resolution, physics, and redshift sampling. They are strong reference solvers and design precedents, not established cheap drop-ins.

**M1 [R13].** Evolves radiation density and flux with a local Eddington closure and conservative finite-volume transport. Per-step work is independent of source count, but the explicit 3D CFL condition requires Δt<Δx/(3c), or a justified reduced c. Figure 1/§3.2 explicitly shows opposing beams failing to cross and producing spurious perpendicular radiation. HLL sharpens beams/shadows but introduces axis asymmetry; GLF diffuses them. Crucially, the paper’s thermochemistry solver conserves photons only to its finite update tolerance even though transport is conservative (§3.3.1). These are implementation-specific errors, not a theorem that all M1 chemistry must violate count conservation. No matched ES runtime factor was verified.

**OTVET and FLD [GA01, PS09].** OTVET constructs an optically thin Eddington tensor and evolves moment equations; the original work advertises N log N scaling independent of source count and conservative number/flux evolution. Petkova–Springel’s implicit anisotropic-diffusion implementation uses a sparse conjugate-gradient solve, and its dense-clump test fails to cast a sharp shadow because the closure ignores intervening attenuation. FLD uses a scalar diffusion closure and is an even weaker candidate for free-streaming shadow/beam problems. Fast conservative transport is therefore possible without correct angular morphology.

**FFT RT [C02].** Cen’s cone/volume convolution method scales as N(log N)² independent of source count. Its reported photon discrepancies are typically 1–5%, reaching 10% in tested cases; this is not exact algebraic conservation. It establishes that FFT-based radiation geometry has substantial prior art, while leaving space for an explicitly balanced allocation formulation.

**Emulators [MPC23, SC26].** GPR/ANN surrogate likelihoods and coarse-to-fine sampling reduce the number of expensive SCRIPT realizations. They do not replace the per-realization conservation problem and are a later optimization route once a physical solver is validated.

## Direct audit of public SCRIPT code

Inspected commit `793c33fd7c252f26ab033d4e00c4dfa594f445a4`, retrieved 2026-10-23 from the author’s Bitbucket API. Reference-only local checkout files are under `/workspace/scratch/40b418003e4a/script_reference`; nothing has been imported into production.

`fortran/pc.f90` contains no FFT. It receives precomputed periodic offsets sorted by distance, with shell degeneracies; source loops stop once their budgets are exhausted. Phase 1 distributes the final incomplete shell equally in photon count per cell. Phase 2 reads the frozen `qi`, accumulates all transfers into `qi_extra`, and updates `qi` only after the round; its incomplete shell is proportional to remaining neutral capacity. The loop repeats while any `qi>1`, without an explicit iteration cap or tolerance. The runtime is controlled by visited source/cell pairs and overlap rounds; worst-case work can be quadratic, even if sparse early cases are cheap.

OpenMP directives in this public file are commented as `!!$OMP`. Its full-saturation shortcut returns `qi=1` when total photons exceed total H without exposing a leftover-photon field. The research contract requires explicitly retaining that residual. Inputs use Fortran default `REAL`; our proposed numerical residual targets must not assume float64-equivalent precision. License is MIT, copyright 2018 Tirthankar Roy Choudhury; reuse requires preserving its notice. Direct reuse was not performed.

## Recommendations and falsification gates

1. **Cheap spatial-budget baseline:** port the *interface* of modern SCRIPT to native 21cmFAST source/sink grids, or implement a separately named equivalent reference. Confirm additive photon units first. Benchmark identical fields, and test source permutation, varying density, finite support, saturation residual, and convergence. This is a useful method even if not novel.
2. **Research candidate:** ES-guided positive-kernel, source-normalized capacity allocation. Compare against SCRIPT, FFRT-P and boundary-distance repair. Its potential distinction must be in a proven allocation rule, locality, or measured cost/accuracy—not simply using FFTs or synchronous updates. Count conservation does not license a physical-RT claim.
3. **Direction-aware candidate/reference:** limited angular channels with adaptive splitting/merging and an explicit surviving-photon ledger. Test the equal-sphere 1/8 surface-crossing cone, unequal sources, opaque obstacles, and rotations before cosmological claims. Preserve angular labels through overlap; isotropically restarting photons at an overlap cell discards the very information the test probes.

All routes require a recombination ledger consistent with the source budget convention, clear UV/X-ray separation, and endogenous evolution tests rather than forcing an externally chosen global history. Power suppression is not a universal theorem of PC: compare same redshift, matched ionized fraction, and identical source/sink fields, then assess morphology and resolution separately.
